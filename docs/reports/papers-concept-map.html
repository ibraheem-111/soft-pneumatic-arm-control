Literature Results Map

How the papers in docs/references.md performed: which modelling or identification method each used, on what robot, with what data, what accuracy it reported, and how far each carries over to our hanging arm (20 pouch pressure inputs, first-order pressure lag τ = 0.6 s, PD baseline 4.10 mm tip RMSE).

11 October 2026 · Built only from the two research agents' paper notes of 7 October 2026 and docs/reports/modeling-options.html; no paper was re-read for this page.

The numbers are not comparable across papers.

Each paper uses a different robot, size, metric and unit: centimetres of average model error (Bruder), millimetres of per-axis tracking RMSE (Papageorgiou), dimensionless NMSE of a bending-angle readout (Hebbal Manjunath), and percent of rod length for a state observer (Zheng). A number only ranks methods inside its own paper. None of them predicts what a method would reach against our 4.10 mm baseline.

What the map says about our arm

  • No accessible paper identifies a pressure lag like ours. Bruder absorbs it in one delay term, Papageorgiou feeds servo counts straight in, the lab's 2022 paper leaves pressure dynamics as future work, Fallahi's title says quasi-static, and Pilch hands it to a separate pressure controller. Della Santina's survey names actuator dynamics as an open challenge. For Falkenhahn and Qiao 2024 it is not accessible. A 0.6 s lag at 100 Hz is 60 samples, so every data-driven option needs measured pouch pressures as states, many delays, or downsampling.
  • No paper has 20 independent inputs. Where the notes give an input count, it is 2 or 3 actuated channels (Zheng uses 2 tendons); Pilch and Falkenhahn's counts are not accessible. Library and lifting methods (SINDYc, Koopman) grow quickly with input count, so we would reduce inputs first, for example to differential column commands.
  • The only paired comparison of a lifted model against a linear state-space model is Bruder's, on a strongly nonlinear foam-skinned arm. Our simulator is close to linear at this scale (see the modelling-options report), so the size of that gap on our plant is unknown.
  • The lab's own papers carry no usable accuracy numbers in our notes. Qiao 2024 numbers were not accessible, and the 2022 notes record no accuracy.

Caveats kept from the notes

  • Falkenhahn 2015: everything beyond metadata comes from a search-engine paraphrase of the abstract and the group's project page; unverified.
  • Fallahi 2026: only the title and one snippet were readable. That it builds on the authors' 2024 Cosserat model is inferred from its reference list.
  • Qiao 2024 and Pilch 2026: abstract (plus snippets for Qiao). How Qiao handled pressure dynamics is not accessible.
  • Papageorgiou 2024: validation reads like free-run simulation but the paper does not say so explicitly. Its robot is cable- and McKibben-driven with servo inputs, not pressure-regulated.
  • Hebbal Manjunath 2026 and Qiao 2024 describe configurations of this lab's arm that differ from the 20 individually regulated pouches in the course slides, and the 2026 paper numbers P1 at the tip, opposite to our simulator.
  • Bruder: one-period free-run error (2021) and 2.5 s-horizon prediction error (2019) are different metrics, so 1.92 cm and 1.59 cm are not directly comparable either.
