Three Arm Models Compared

All three models start from the same rigid-chain Lagrangian, \(M\ddot q + C\dot q + D\dot q + Kq + g(q) = S\,p\), with the pressure entering through a pneumatic actuator. They differ in which terms they keep, which they hand to an observer, and which they drop. That choice sets each model's order, its poles and what controller it can support.

Models compared: (1) Nikoletta's reduced model in SMC_Controller_withoutdisturbanceobserver.ipynb, ported as scripts/smc_reduced_model.py. (2) The "Spring–Damper Arm Model" page, docs/reports/spring-damper-model.html, whose own numbers are illustrative; here it is evaluated with our simulator's parameters. (3) Qiao, Tao & Zhang, IFAC-PapersOnLine 58(28) 516–521, 2024, read in full. Numbers marked "sim" are calculated from build_arm_xml(ArmConfig()) for the x-bending chain about the straight hang.

Read down the gravity column and the difference is clear. The notebook and the paper both leave gravity out of the model, but only the paper has something, the observer, that is meant to absorb it. The spring–damper page is the only model that keeps the lag, the term that dominates our simulator.

Model 3 · Qiao, Tao & Zhang 2024

First-order LPV plus a lumped disturbance

\[ \dot q = A(\rho)\,q + B(\rho)\,u + d, \quad A = -\operatorname{diag}\!\Big(\tfrac{k_i(\rho)}{c_i(\rho)}\Big) \]

The arm is light, so inertia, Coriolis and gravity go into \(d\), leaving a massless spring–damper per configuration variable \(q = [\theta_j, L_j]\). \(k, c\) are quadratic in a pressure-derived \(\rho\). The input is a torque or force through an inner pressure loop assumed fast.

What each gets right, and where it fails on our plant

Notebook

  • Right: an exact modal derivation from the simulator, the correct kinematic Jacobian, and mode selection by static tip gain.
  • Fails: gravity stiffness is missing (static gain 7.74 vs 1.80 mm/psi), the lag and delay are missing (relative degree 2 vs 3 plus dead time), and nothing estimates what is missing.

Spring–damper page

  • Right: it keeps the two terms that dominate our simulator, gravity and the lag. It also notes that 4 pouches per level make only 3 forces, and that hanging makes \(K_g\) stabilizing.
  • Fails: it gives no treatment of uncertainty and no controller. The delay and the pressure-dependent stiffness appear only as later fixes. Its interactive parameters are illustrative: with our simulator's values the first mode has ζ = 0.66, not "lightly damped".

Qiao, Tao & Zhang

  • Right: it keeps the model deliberately small, puts the error where an observer can estimate it, schedules the stiffness on pressure, and proves exponential convergence of the error.
  • Fails here: it assumes the actuator is fast. Our 0.6 s lag costs 20.7° of phase at 0.1 Hz, and the delay another 18° (calculated). The paper does not consider a dead time. Its state also needs configuration sensing that motion capture of the tip alone does not give.

Reading them together

The three models are not competitors so much as answers to different questions. The spring–damper page asks what the plant is, the paper asks how to control it despite a crude model, and the notebook sits between them without the observer that would make the paper's simplifications safe. The pole chart suggests the outline of a combined model. It would keep the paper's first-order structure and its observer, use an effective stiffness that includes gravity (its slow poles then match the simulator exactly), and add the lag and delay the page keeps. That combination has not been built or tested. It is a direction, not a result.
