Arm Controller Comparison

Eight controllers in three families, scored the same way: the package's own evaluate_controller on the 3-input calibrated plant. They are the notebook's PD and reduced-model SMC, the Qiao, Tao & Zhang SMC and NDOSMC on a model identified from sensor data, and a delay-aware version of the paper's controller. The result is clear-cut: modelling the actuator's 0.5 s dead time and 0.6 s lag matters far more than anything else.

Scored task: CircularTrackingTask() (7 mm circle, 0.1 Hz, 5 s settle, 30 s scored) on make_calibrated_sim(seed=0). Held-out check: 10 mm circle at 0.15 Hz on seed 5. All numbers below were measured with uv run python scripts/compare_controllers.py on branch ndosmc. The model behind the paper and delay-aware controllers is in docs/reports/task-space-model.html.

RMSE is of the lateral (x–y) error in mm over the whole scored window, as the harness reports it. "After 5 s" drops the opening transient. Every run's maximum error is 7.00 mm or more, because the circle starts 7 mm from the settled tip. Phase lag is the harness's cross-correlation on y, positive when the tip lags. Effort is the RMS of the commands minus the 4.5 psi baseline.

What each family does

Notebook: PD and the reduced-model SMC

Task-space PD and an SMC on a one-mode spring–damper model derived from the simulator's own parameters. The derivation omits gravity and the actuator, and the allocator rotates x and y by 90°. Correcting the allocator alone takes PD from 9.94 to 4.08 mm and the SMC from 4.37 to 3.20 mm.

\[ \Delta p = \tfrac1b\big[kx + c\dot x + \ddot x_d - \lambda\dot e - K\,\mathrm{sat}(s/\phi)\big],\quad s = \dot e + \lambda e \]

Paper: SMC and NDOSMC on M1

The paper's first-order model in task space, \(c_i\dot X_i + k_iX_i = v_i\), \(v = Gu\), identified from sensor data alone. The actuator is assumed ideal, as in the paper, so the 0.5 s delay and 0.6 s lag end up inside one slow pole (\(1/a \approx 1.44\) s). The pressure scheduling was implemented, but it changes nothing: LTI and LPV both give 1.46 mm.

\[ v_i = c_i\big(\dot X_{d,i} + \tfrac{k_i}{c_i}X_i - \lambda e_i - \eta_i\,\mathrm{sat}(e_i/\mu) - \hat d_i\big),\quad u = G^{-1}v \]

The NDOB uses \(\dot z = g(-aX - bu - \hat d)\), \(g = l_1 + 2l_2|X|\). Two printed signs in the paper are corrected here: \(B = +1/c\), and \(-\hat d\) in eq. 7.

Delay-aware: predictor + lag inverse (+ NDOB)

The same per-axis law, now on M2: the mechanics identified from measured pressure, \(a = (4.77, 4.70, 15.5)\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\), plus the identified actuator \(g_j e^{-0.5s}/(0.6s+1)\). Three changes: a Smith predictor feeds the law with \(\hat X(t+T)\), the reference is extrapolated \(T\) ahead, and the lag is inverted.

\[ \hat X(t{+}T) = X(t) + X_m(t) - X_m(t{-}T),\qquad u_j = \tfrac{1}{g_j}\big(p_{\mathrm{des},j} + \tau\,\dot p_{\mathrm{des},j}\big) \]

Reading the results

  • The actuator model is the decisive ingredient. Moving from M1, where the actuator is assumed ideal, to M2 with a predictor takes steady-state error from 0.87 to 0.09 mm on the scored task, and from 3.08 to 0.98 mm on the held-out one. That matches the model report, where M2's free-run error was 4–6× lower than M1's.
  • The paper's observer adds little here, and can hurt. On M1, the NDOSMC was worse than the plain SMC on the scored task (2.84 vs 1.46 mm) and slightly better on the held-out task (3.19 vs 3.45 mm). The observer estimates a "disturbance" that is mostly the controller's own input arriving 0.5 s late, so its correction is always late. With the delay modelled, it changes the result by 0.02–0.03 mm.
  • The paper's plain SMC already beats every notebook controller. Its model is identified from data, so its gain and time constant are measured, not derived with gravity missing. Its feedforward term \(c\,\dot X_d\) supplies phase lead that partly offsets the unmodelled delay at 0.1 Hz.
  • Better tracking costs more pressure. The delay-aware controllers use about 2.1 psi RMS against PD's 1.0 psi. Most of that goes to closing the initial 7 mm gap quickly and to inverting the lag.
  • Saturation, as the share of command samples at 0 or 9 psi (same runs). On the scored task it is 0.0–0.2 % for every controller except two: the notebook's PD as written (8.5 %) and the paper NDOSMC (31.0 %). The NDOSMC's observer drives the commands into the limits, which is part of why it does worse than the plain SMC. On the held-out task the model-based controllers saturate heavily: paper SMC 43.3 %, paper NDOSMC 52.9 %, delay-aware 21.2–21.3 %. The notebook PD and SMC with the corrected allocator are at 0.0–0.5 %. A 10 mm circle at 0.15 Hz partly exceeds what 0–9 psi can deliver through the lag, so the held-out numbers are partly limited by the actuator, not only by the controller.

Caveats

  • Gains were tuned on the scored task itself. The sweeps (every run is printed by scripts/ndosmc.py --sweep and scripts/smc_reduced_model.py --sweep) used seed 0, the same as Nikoletta's tuning. The held-out task tests generalization. It keeps the ranking but roughly doubles every error, partly because 0.15 Hz is harder through a 0.5 s delay and partly because of saturation (see above). No anti-windup was added beyond feeding the clipped command to the observer and predictor.
  • The models were identified without simulator internals, using the tip with added 0.1 mm noise and the pressure sensors. The controllers read the harness's clean tip_pos and tip_vel, which correspond to motion-capture position and velocity on hardware. Measurement noise was not added during the scored runs.
  • Simulation only. On hardware the regulator may not be a clean dead time plus lag, and the delay may vary. A Smith predictor is sensitive to delay mismatch; this was not tested.
  • The benchmark question is still open. All of this uses the package harness and the 3-input plant, not the make_sim() 20-pouch benchmark in AGENTS.md.
