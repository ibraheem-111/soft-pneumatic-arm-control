Pouch Arm Modelling Options

Analytical and data-driven ways to build a model of the plant (the 20 pouch pressure commands in, the tip position out), on the real robot or in the MuJoCo simulator. It reviews the papers in docs/references.md and lists what each method would need from us. It deliberately makes no selection; section 7 lists the questions that decide it.

1. What the model has to capture

The simulator implements this signal chain, read from its source and checked by running it:

  • The lag is as slow as the mechanics. τ = 0.6 s (corner ≈ 0.27 Hz) is comparable to the slowest mechanical poles (about −1.25 s⁻¹, i.e. 0.8 s), so it can't be neglected. The order from command to tip is at least 3 per axis: 1 lag state plus about 2 mechanical states.
  • 20 inputs act as about 15 directions. Opposing columns cancel in bending, so the commands reduce to 10 bending moments (x and y at 5 levels) plus 5 axial forces.
  • The simulator is nearly linear at this scale. A linearization about 4.5 psi matched the nonlinear simulator to 0.1 % (tip error 0.004 mm RMS against 4.33 mm of motion) for opposing columns driven over 0–9 psi (scripts/plant_model.py). That check reads the simulator's internal state, so it serves only as an answer key, not as a modelling method.
  • The real arm is described as nonlinear. The lab's own 2026 paper calls the hardware "strongly nonlinear, hysteretic, and configuration-dependent". A method chosen only because the simulator is linear may not transfer.

2. What data we can get

Simulator
Any experiment we like, at about 11× real time. Tip position and velocity, plus the 20 pouch pressure sensors (curvature coupling plus noise σ = 0.002 psi). The tip sensors are noise-free, so add measurement noise for realism. p_actual and the joint state q are also exposed but don't exist on hardware.
Real robot, existing
36 recorded runs (upstream main branch, data/_data_extract/): axial, circular and triangular waveforms at 0.1 Hz, 1–10 psi. Signals: segment-level commands, pouch pressures, OptiTrack at about 50 Hz. The lab fitted the simulator to these runs. At 0.1 Hz the excitation sits well below the 0.27 Hz lag corner, so these runs show the static gain and slow behaviour but say little about the dynamics.
Real robot, new
Only if the lab gives us time on the arm. Its configuration is unclear: see section 8.

3. Analytical (physics-based) methods

Write the equations from mechanics, then estimate a small set of physical parameters from experiments. All of these still need data; what differs is how much structure is fixed in advance.

A1 · Low-order gray-box lag + mass–spring–damper

Structure
Per bending axis: a first-order lag on pressure, then a second-order mechanical response, G(s) = K ωₙ² / ((τs + 1)(s² + 2ζωₙs + ωₙ²)). The lag can be fitted on its own from the measured pouch pressures.
Parameters
τ, gain K, ωₙ, ζ per axis, plus how each pouch level maps to each axis.
Experiments
Steps and multisines on opposing-column differences. Fitting by prediction-error or output-error least squares.
In the papers
The lab's 2022 one-segment work fitted a rigid-arm model M θ̈ + C θ̇ + G + Kθ + Dθ̇ = αR(ψ)p with lsqnonlin on ten-sinusoid inputs (0.001–0.1 Hz). It did not model pressure dynamics.
Strengths
Few parameters, physically meaningful, a direct transfer function or state-space model for linear control and SMC.
Limits
Assumes the response is dominated by one mechanical mode. In the simulator, the Hankel singular values (1, 0.24, 0.09, 0.04 per axis) suggest a few more states matter.

A2 · Piecewise constant curvature + Euler–Lagrange PCC

Structure
Each section bends as a circular arc; the configuration is a bending angle and plane (and length) per section; M(q)q̈ + C q̇ + G(q) + D q̇ + K q = A(q) p.
Parameters
Stiffness and damping per section, pressure-to-moment gain; geometry from the drawings.
In the papers
Pilch et al. 2026: Euler–Lagrange with an experimentally identified spring-moment model that depends on curvature, orientation and pressure; IMU state; linearized around the reference for feedforward plus PID; an external pressure controller realizes the pressures. Qiao et al. 2024 (same lab, earlier two-segment arm): PCC reduced to a first-order LPV model. Falkenhahn et al. 2015: lumped mass per section with feedback linearization (paper not accessible).
Strengths
Standard in the continuum-robot literature; interpretable; extends to nonlinear control.
Limits
The bending-plane parametrization has singularities at straight configurations (Della Santina survey), which is exactly where a hanging arm rests. The simulator is 5 rigid links, not arcs, so PCC will not match it exactly. Pressure dynamics must be added separately.

A3 · Rigid-link chain with fitted parameters pseudo-rigid body

Structure
Links joined by spring–damper joints, which the survey calls "a standard rigid robot with parallel elasticity". This is the simulator's own structure: 5 levels × (1 slide + 2 hinges).
In the papers / lab
The lab calibrated the simulator this way: bounded differential evolution, fitted on 6 of the 36 real runs, validated on all 36. Mechanics replay gave 3.03 mm lateral RMSE, and the full end-to-end replay 3.34 mm.
Strengths
Proven on this exact robot; captures gravity and geometry; plugs into MuJoCo.
Limits
Fitting it in simulation would just recover the parameters the simulator was built from. It is a high-order nonlinear model (50 states with the lag), so controllers need a reduced version. Global optimization is slow.

A4 · Cosserat rod continuum PDE

Structure
Partial differential equations for pose, strain and internal wrench along the arm's length.
In the papers
Zheng and Burgner-Kahrs 2026 use full Cosserat dynamics for a state observer on a tendon-driven steel rod (position error 0.9–1.9 % of length). Fallahi et al. 2026 likely build on their 2024 Cosserat model of a 3-chamber pneumatic arm (quasi-static, simulation-only SMC); that link is inferred, not verified.
Strengths
The most faithful continuum mechanics.
Limits
The survey calls it "hard to use directly for control". Our fabric arm has no clear backbone, and the pressure-to-wrench map would itself need identifying. Very high effort for a course project.

A5 · Finite elements + model order reduction FEM / MOR

Structure
A volumetric mesh of the fabric and pouches, then a projection to a few dozen modes (POD or modal analysis).
Available
The upstream repo has a separate SOFA simulation (soft-arm-sofa/) that includes SOFA's ModelOrderReduction plugin.
Limits
The survey lists nonlinear K(q), non-constant damping and actuation, and up to hundreds of thousands of states before reduction. It models a different simulator from the one the course uses, and the effort is very high.

4. Data-driven methods

Choose a model class, then fit it to input/output records (commands in; pressures and tip position out). They work the same way on the simulator and on hardware, provided the excitation is rich enough.

D1 · Linear transfer-function models ARX · ARMAX · OE · PEM

Structure
Difference equations A(q)y = B(q)u + noise model. ARX is plain least squares. Output-error and prediction-error fits minimize free-run simulation error, which is the metric that matters here.
Experiments
PRBS or multisine on differential inputs around a bias, 0.02–3 Hz. Order chosen by validation error or AIC.
Strengths
The textbook course method (Ljung). Fast, with mature tools: MATLAB System Identification Toolbox, sippy, or plain NumPy for ARX.
Limits
Linear only. With 20 inputs the parameter count grows quickly unless the inputs are first reduced to differentials.

D2 · Subspace state-space identification N4SID · MOESP

Structure
Gives (A, B, C, D) directly from block-Hankel matrices of the data. The model order is read off the singular values.
In the papers
Bruder et al. used a 4th-order subspace model as their linear baseline: 4.74 cm average prediction error (T-RO 2021) and 3.63 cm (2019), against 1.6–1.9 cm for Koopman, on a McKibben arm with a foam skin.
Strengths
Handles multi-input, multi-output systems naturally. The output plugs straight into LQR, LQG, MPC or an observer.
Limits
Linear. Bruder's comparison shows the gap on a strongly nonlinear arm. How large the gap is for ours is unknown.

D3 · Frequency-domain identification multisine · FRF

Structure
Estimate the frequency response at the excited frequencies, then fit a rational transfer function.
Strengths
You see the lag corner, the mechanical modes and the x–y coupling before committing to an order. Odd-multisine designs (Pintelon and Schoukens) also measure how much the response departs from linear, which is useful given the real arm's reported nonlinearity.
Limits
Needs periodic, steady-state experiments. Each input channel needs its own experiment or orthogonal multisines.

D4 · Linear parameter-varying models LPV

Structure
A family of linear models whose coefficients depend on a scheduling variable such as bias pressure or bend angle.
In the papers
Qiao et al. 2024 (same lab): "The stiffness and damping coefficients were identified at each set point independently, followed by the application of a linear least squares method"; a first-order polynomial fit "results in degraded predictive accuracy". Used with SMC and a fast nonlinear disturbance observer.
Strengths
Captures operating-point dependence while staying linear in form; the lab already uses it.
Limits
Worth the effort only if the gains really change across operating points, which needs testing. Their model is first order, with no pressure lag.

D5 · Sparse identification with control SINDYc

Structure
ẋ = Ξ Θ(x, u): sparse regression over a library of candidate terms (polynomials, trigonometric functions, cross terms between state and input). The result is short, readable equations.
In the papers
Papageorgiou et al. 2024, on a cable- and McKibben-driven module with servo inputs: first-order model in tip position, polynomials up to degree 2, 652,000 samples. Fit 83.7–94.7 % per axis. Super-twisting SMC gave 0.77–0.94 mm RMSE on a 35 mm circle, against 1.23–2.31 mm for PI.
Strengths
An interpretable nonlinear model in a form ready for SMC, and the closest published "identify, then SMC" template.
Limits
Needs derivatives of noisy data. The library grows rapidly with 20 inputs. Their model has no actuator lag; ours would need pressure states, or the measured pressure as the input. Closed-loop data makes the regression ill-conditioned (Brunton et al.).

D6 · Koopman operator / EDMD lifted linear

Structure
Lift the state (with delays) through basis functions, then fit a linear model in the lifted space: z⁺ = Az + Bu, x = Cz. Linear MPC then becomes a convex quadratic program.
In the papers
Bruder et al. 2021, hanging 2-section pneumatic arm, 3 inputs: 45,103 snapshot pairs, 36 degree-2 basis functions, one delay. Linear Koopman 1.92 cm prediction error against 4.74 cm for a subspace model; K-MPC tracking 0.46 cm against 3.45 cm for linear MPC.
Strengths
Captures nonlinearity while keeping linear-control tools. The robot class is closest to ours.
Limits
Choosing the basis is an art, and its size grows fast (330 functions at degree 4 in the 2019 paper). One delay can't represent a 0.6 s lag at 100 Hz (60 samples): it needs measured pressures in the state, many delays, or downsampling. Less natural for SMC than an explicit ODE.

D7 · Neural-network models NARX · LSTM

In the papers
Bruder's NARX baseline had 3.18 cm error, between the linear and Koopman models. The survey groups learned models with MPC and RL.
Strengths
Flexible; no structure needed.
Limits
Needs a lot of data, is opaque, and is hard to use for SMC design or stability proofs.

D8 · Hybrid: physics or linear model + learned residual gray-box + GP / NN

Structure
Keep a simple model (A1 or D2) and learn only its prediction error with a Gaussian process or a small network.
Strengths
Keeps the interpretable core, and the residual size is a direct measure of model uncertainty, which is what an SMC switching gain or disturbance observer needs.
Limits
Not demonstrated in the papers reviewed here. Two layers to validate.
Related but not a plant model: physical reservoir computing.

Hebbal Manjunath et al. 2026 (the simulator's author) decode the arm's bending angle from delayed pouch pressures with a linear ridge readout. Sealed pouches gave median NMSE 0.148, against 0.842 with a shared manifold, and two sensors carry most of the information. The work supports using pouch pressures as extra measurements (for observers or augmented states), but it gives no lag, gain or dynamics.

6. Paper notes

Read by two research agents on 7 October 2026. The access level is stated for each paper, and nothing marked "abstract" or "not accessible" should be cited for details beyond what is quoted here.

Abstract only

Pilch, Menrad, Beger, Sawodny · 2026

Dynamic modeling and control of an inextensible pneumatically actuated soft continuum manipulator. Mechatronics 115, 103462.

Euler–Lagrange model with an experimentally identified spring-moment model (curvature, orientation and pressure dependent); state from IMUs in azimuth/zenith angles. Pressures come from a linear map of target moments, plus a mean-pressure offset, and are realized by "an external, model-free pressure controller". Linearized about the reference for feedforward plus PID. No numbers were accessible.

Title and one snippet only

Fallahi, Zareinejad, Rezaei, Ghafarirad, Talebi · 2026

Adaptive sliding mode control for quasi-static position tracking of a pneumatic soft manipulator. European Journal of Control 91, 101548.

Closed access. The title gives adaptive SMC under model uncertainty, for quasi-static tracking. The authors' 2024 predecessor uses a Cosserat rod model of a 3-chamber pneumatic arm, with SMC tested only in simulation; that the 2026 paper builds on it is likely but unverified. A quasi-static model would not capture our 0.6 s lag.

Full text

Papageorgiou, Sigurðardóttir, Falotico, Tolu · 2024

Sliding-mode control of a soft robot based on data-driven sparse identification. Control Engineering Practice 144, 105836.

Cable- and McKibben-driven module; inputs are servo commands (300–500 counts), not pressures. SINDYc with a first-order model in tip position, polynomials up to degree 2 (trigonometric terms discarded), 652,000 samples, 80/20 split, fit 83.7 % (x), 85.1 % (y), 94.7 % (z). Cascade super-twisting SMC with feedback linearization and an adaptive input inversion, proved UGAS. Circle RMSE 0.94/0.88/0.77 mm against PI 2.31/2.29/1.23 mm. Its "MAE" column exceeds RMSE, so it is probably maximum error.

Abstract + snippets; 2022 predecessor in full

Qiao, Tao, Zhang · 2024

Nonlinear disturbance observer with sliding mode control for a fabric soft robotic arm. IFAC-PapersOnLine 58(28), 516–521.

Same lab, two-segment arm: front/back columns held at 2 psi, left/right modulated. First-order LPV model under PCC, coefficients identified per set point by least squares, then polynomial fits. Fast nonlinear disturbance observer plus SMC, proved asymptotically stable; RMSE reduced over 7 trials (numbers not accessible). The 2022 one-segment paper used SMC ITV1050 regulators, OptiTrack at 120 Hz and 100 Hz control, and a lsqnonlin grey-box fit; it states that pressure dynamics were not modelled.

Full text

Bruder, Fu, Gillespie, Remy, Vasudevan · 2021 (and 2019 conference version)

Data-driven control of soft robots using Koopman operator theory. IEEE T-RO 37(3). · arXiv:1902.02827.

Hanging 2-section McKibben arm with a foam skin, 3 regulator inputs, laser-dot tip measurement, Ts = 0.083 s. EDMD with LASSO, degree-2 monomials (N = 36) and one delay; random ramp-and-hold excitation; held-out sinusoid validation. Prediction error: linear Koopman 1.92 cm, nonlinear Koopman 1.61 cm, subspace 4.74 cm, NARX 3.18 cm. Tracking: K-MPC 0.46 cm against linear MPC 3.45 cm. Pneumatics were not modelled explicitly.

Full text

Brunton, Proctor, Kutz · 2016

SINDy (PNAS 113) and SINDYc (arXiv:1605.06682).

Sparse regression over a library including state–input cross terms; derivatives by total-variation regularization; sparsity chosen at the Pareto knee or by cross-validation. Shows that DMDc and Koopman-with-inputs fit the same regression framework. Warns that feedback-only data (u = k(x)) makes the regression ill-conditioned, so inject dither. Examples are Lotka–Volterra and Lorenz; no robots.

Full text

Hebbal Manjunath, Wang, Li, Zhang · 2026

Towards effective physical reservoir computing with a pneumatic soft robot. arXiv:2609.02157.

This lab's arm: Segments 2–4 driven (u ∈ ℝ³), Segment 1 a passive 5-pouch sensing column. 100 Hz; OptiTrack. Same 36-run, 0.1 Hz data family. Linear ridge readout of bending angle from 0.2 s of delayed pressures. Notes that pneumatic transmission low-pass filters the response at higher frequencies. Numbers pouches P1 at the end effector.

Full text

Della Santina, Duriez, Rus · 2023

Model-based control of soft robots: a survey. IEEE Control Systems Magazine 43(3).

All finite-dimensional models share M(q)q̈ + C q̇ + G + D q̇ + K = A(q)τ. Taxonomy: rigid-part-dominant, Cosserat, piecewise constant strain (PCC, rigid-link), functional bases, FEM, order reduction, learning. Distinguishes simulation models (accurate) from control models (low-dimensional). Actuator dynamics: a coupled actuator equation, handled by singular perturbation or backstepping. Lists robust SMC under robust methods.

Full text (arXiv v2)

Zheng, Burgner-Kahrs · 2026

Estimating dynamic soft continuum robot states from boundaries. IJRR.

Cosserat-rod boundary observers using base wrench or tip velocity; tendon-driven steel rod; position error 0.9–1.9 % of length. The practical lesson for us is tip feedback in an observer; the finite-dimensional equivalent is a Luenberger observer or EKF on whichever model we identify.

Abstract / not accessible

Webster and Jones · 2010; Falkenhahn et al. · 2015

Constant-curvature kinematics review (IJRR 29). · Model-based feedforward via feedback linearization (ICRA 2015).

Webster: PCC splits into a robot-specific map (actuators to arc parameters) and a general map (arcs to pose). Falkenhahn: only metadata could be read; the survey cites it as a purely feedforward dynamic controller with one lumped mass per section. Get both through the library before citing details.

7. Questions before selecting

The comparison doesn't single out one method until these are answered:

  1. Simulator only, or must it transfer to the real arm? In simulation the plant is nearly linear, which favours A1, D1–D3. A real arm described as hysteretic and configuration-dependent would favour D4–D6 or D8.
  2. Which real configuration? 20 independent pouches, or 3 driven segments plus a sealed column (section 8). This decides how many inputs the model has.
  3. What will the model be used for? SMC wants explicit equations and a known relative degree (A1, A2, D4, D5). MPC is happy with any state-space or lifted-linear model (D2, D6).
  4. What does the course expect? Identification from input/output data (D1–D3), or a physics derivation (A1–A2)? Interpretability matters for the written report.
  5. How much time? D1–D3 and A1 take days; D5, D6 and A2 take weeks; A4 and A5 are beyond the course.

8. Corrections found during review

Real-arm configuration conflict.

The lab's 2026 paper and the upstream calibration describe 3 driven segments plus a sealed sensing column; the lab slides describe 20 individually regulated pouches. Both papers number P1 at the end effector, while our simulator puts P1 at the mount. Recorded as an open question in docs/hardware.md.

  • Qiao et al. 2024 used a two-segment arm, not ours. The claims in our references that it "assumes the inner pressure loop is fast enough to ignore", and its licence, are unverified; docs/references.md is corrected.
  • Papageorgiou et al. 2024 is cable- and McKibben-driven with servo inputs, not pressure-regulated; noted in references.
  • The lab's existing real data is at 0.1 Hz only, below the lag corner, so it cannot identify the fast dynamics.
