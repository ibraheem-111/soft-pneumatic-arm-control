Lecture notes · modelling a hanging pneumatic arm

Yes, it is a chain of springs and dampers driven through a lag

A soft arm looks continuous, but for control we only need the few motions that the inputs excite and the tip sensor sees. Lump each pouch level into a rigid link, put a spring and a damper at every joint, add gravity, and feed the joints through a first-order pneumatic lag. That gives a model you can derive on paper, linearize, and break into independent mass–spring–dampers.

1. The equivalent mechanism

Replace each of the n pouch levels with a rigid link of length ℓ and mass m. Each link connects to the one above through a joint with three motions: two bends (θx, θy) and one stretch (e). Every motion has a spring and a damper across it. That pairing is a Kelvin–Voigt element: the spring stores elastic energy in the fabric and the damper dissipates it.

2. Writing it down: Euler–Lagrange

Collect the coordinates as q = [θx₁, θy₁, e₁, …, θxₙ, θyₙ, eₙ]. Write the energies, the dissipation and the work done by pressure, and the equations follow mechanically.

kinetic T = ½ q̇ᵀ M(q) q̇ M from link masses and the chain geometry elastic V_e = ½ Σᵢ [ k_b (θxᵢ² + θyᵢ²) + k_a eᵢ² ] gravity V_g = −g Σᵢ mᵢ zᵢ(q) z measured downward from the mount dissipation R = ½ Σᵢ [ c_b (θ̇xᵢ² + θ̇yᵢ²) + c_a ėᵢ² ] input work δW = (S p)ᵀ δq S maps pouch pressures to joint moments and axial forces d/dt(∂T/∂q̇) − ∂T/∂q + ∂R/∂q̇ + ∂(V_e + V_g)/∂q = S p M(q) q̈ + C(q, q̇) q̇ + D q̇ + K q + g(q) = S p mechanics τ ṗ = −p + u pneumatics

Each term has a plain physical meaning. M is inertia, which couples the levels: moving a level near the mount drags everything below it. C collects the centrifugal and Coriolis terms. D and K are the dampers and springs, and they are diagonal in these coordinates. g(q) is gravity. S is a fixed geometric matrix: at each level, the difference between opposite pouches makes a bending moment (moment arm r), and their sum makes an axial force.

Look at S closely. Four pouches per level produce only three generalized forces: two moments and one axial force. So one combination of pressures per level does nothing to the mechanics. That is a modelling fact you can see before touching any data.

3. Linearize about the hanging pose

At small deflections about the straight-down equilibrium, the cos and sin terms in M and g go to first order and C drops out. Gravity contributes a stiffness K_g. For a hanging arm it is positive definite, because a pendulum hanging down is pulled back toward the vertical. An upright arm would get −K_g instead, which is why upright soft arms buckle and hanging ones do not.

M₀ q̈ + D q̇ + (K + K_g) q = S p τ ṗ = −p + u small angles decouple the three directions: x-bending chain (n DOF) · y-bending chain (n DOF) · axial chain (n DOF) tip, planar, small angles: x_tip ≈ ℓ Σᵢ (n − i + 1) θxᵢ

The tip formula explains a fact that surprises students: a pouch next to the mount moves the tip more than one at the tip. A bend at level 1 swings all n links below it, while a bend at level n swings only the last one. The leverage ratio is n : 1 geometrically, reduced somewhat by how the springs share the load.

So the answer to your question is yes, at three levels of fidelity. At the coarsest, one mass–spring–damper per bending axis behind a lag, which is three parameters plus τ and a gain. At the next level, the n-link chain, which is the same thing with n modes. Higher modes are stiffer and are hard to excite through a 0.6 s-type lag, so they rarely matter for tip tracking. 7. Where a linear spring–damper picture breaks

These are the effects that make a real fabric arm differ from the clean model. Each has a standard fix that keeps the spring–damper skeleton.

EffectWhat you would seeFix that keeps the structure
Viscoelastic fabricSlow creep after a step; hysteresis loops between loading and unloadingReplace Kelvin–Voigt with a standard linear solid (spring in parallel with a spring–damper in series). It adds one internal state per joint.
Pressure-dependent stiffnessThe arm gets stiffer as the mean pressure rises; ω and gain shift with the operating pointMake k a function of mean pressure, k(p̄), and schedule the linear model on p̄ (an LPV model).
Large deflectionsGain drops at the edges of the workspaceKeep the nonlinear M(q) and g(q) instead of the linearized model.
Pressure can only pushSaturation at 0 and at the regulator limitBias every pouch and work in differences, and put the bounds into the controller (MPC or anti-windup).
Regulator dynamics are not first orderRate limits, a dead time, asymmetric fill and ventAdd a delay or a second-order regulator model in front of the lag.

What to identify, in order: τ from a pressure step, with the pressure sensors alone. Then ω, ζ and the gain b from a tip step, using the measured pressure as the input. Then decide, from the residual, whether the second mode or any of the effects above earn a place in the model.
